Context

Problem

An invited group needs a simple voting experience while the server preserves identity, final-ballot consistency, and a recoverable count.

Outcome

A moderator can conduct the documented poll from preparation to outcome, and a participant invited through several addresses contributes one revisable vote.

Scope

In scope
Configured moderator login; draft/edit/copy/open; grouped contacts; invitations; participant links/sessions; ranking and revision; pause/resume/close; automatic deadline processing; count persistence; results; optional email announcement; help; delivery status.
Out of scope
Public enrolment, password accounts, public results, individual-ballot inspection, election administration, arbitrary surveys, live infrastructure deployment, bulk historical import, and a cross-project shared framework.
Unchanged

Decisions

The following first-release policies were approved with this specification on 8 September 2026. README/VISION now reflect these decisions. The original proposal wording remains in Git and the dated audit history; it is not a current request to choose those policies again.

Area Approved policy
Moderator authority Each moderator administers only their own polls and copies only their own lists. An invited moderator may vote through a separate participant identity.
Visibility The owner sees aggregate turnout, count detail, winners, and contact/delivery administration. No interface directly exposes another person's ballot or voting status. Small-poll results may permit inference; disclose this before voting, without a minimum-group-size restriction. A voter sees only their own current ballot.
Open-poll editing Opening freezes question, options, winner count, deadline, participant grouping, and announcement setting. Corrections use a new draft copied from the old poll; no existing vote is silently reassigned.
Pause and deadline Resume is available before the original deadline. The deadline closes both open and paused polls. Manual close is offered from paused state; closed polls never reopen.
Acceptance time Take the server's UTC time after obtaining the write transaction. Accept only while open and strictly before the stored deadline.
Deadline entry Enter a date/time with an explicit UTC offset; display UTC and the entered offset. Do not guess a timezone or ambiguous daylight-saving time.
Invalid ballots Require at least one preference and consecutive unique ranks from 1. Reject gaps, ties, unknown options, or non-integer ranks without changing the last valid ballot.
Contact ambiguity Trim and compare email addresses case-insensitively; de-duplicate within a participant, reject the same normalized address across participants in one poll. No inference joins separately entered people.
Announcement Checkbox defaults off. If selected, send the short result email below to every invited address; no public result page is created.
Zero turnout Close with a no-votes outcome and no winners; announce that outcome only if selected. Do not call W002 with empty input.
Returning access Participant links are reusable for 24 hours; moderator links are one-use for 15 minutes. Sessions last 12 hours. Eligible users can request a fresh link. State/deadline checks always remain authoritative.
Retention Keep polls, grouped contacts, final ballots, count records, and delivery outcomes for reuse and review. No automatic deletion of these poll records or anonymous-ballot promise is introduced. Expired technical sessions and rate-limit entries are cleaned up as specified below.
Input bounds At most 1000 participants, 8 addresses each, 50 options, and 8 MiB per form body; text bounds are defined below.
Delivery timing Declare a one-minute sweep interval. Announcement work is eligible when the result commits; normal processing attempts it in the same sweep if within the execution bound.

These choices are approved requirements. Changes require explicit amendment; the product documents preserve their original questions and resolutions.

Acceptance Criteria

AC003.1 - Configured moderator access

Criterion
Only a currently configured moderator can authenticate for administration. Authentication never grants authority over another moderator's polls.
Proven by
RT003.1 - Moderator authentication and ownership.

AC003.2 - Create and prepare a poll

Criterion
An authenticated moderator can save and edit a draft poll with question, options, winner count, participants, deadline, and announcement preference, and receives its unique URL.
Proven by
RT003.2 - Draft creation and validation.

AC003.3 - Several addresses share one participant

Criterion
Comma- or colon-separated addresses entered together belong to one participant. Every address-specific access grant reaches that same participant and ballot.
Proven by
RT003.3 - Address grouping and entitlement.

AC003.4 - Reuse a previous electorate

Criterion
Copying an owned poll's participant list preserves address groups and permits draft edits while leaving the source electorate and ballots unchanged.
Proven by
RT003.4 - Copy and edit an electorate.

AC003.5 - Invite every address once logically

Criterion
Opening a valid draft records an invitation for every listed address and opens voting atomically. Repeating the opening action creates no additional logical invitations.
Proven by
RT003.5 - Opening and invitation recovery.

AC003.6 - Short invitation copy

Criterion
Each invitation uses the exact short copy in VISION, substituting only the poll subject and recipient's link; the message is addressed individually.
Proven by
RT003.5 - Opening and invitation recovery; UT003.1 - Voting and help experience.

AC003.7 - Passwordless participant access

Criterion
A valid participant link authenticates its poll participant without a password. Invalid, expired, wrong-purpose, or revoked access cannot expose or modify a ballot.
Proven by
RT003.6 - Participant grant and session boundaries.

AC003.8 - Rank a subset of options

Criterion
A participant can submit consecutive unique preferences from 1 onwards, leaving other options unranked. Invalid rankings return usable errors without changing the accepted ballot.
Proven by
RT003.7 - Ballot validation and feedback.

AC003.9 - Unlimited replacement of one vote

Criterion
While voting is open and before the deadline, a participant can replace the current ballot repeatedly through any associated address, retaining one effective vote.
Proven by
RT003.8 - Revision and concurrent aliases.

AC003.10 - Stale submissions preserve newer votes

Criterion
A submission based on an older ballot version does not overwrite a newer accepted ballot. The voter receives a conflict and can reload the current preferences.
Proven by
RT003.8 - Revision and concurrent aliases.

AC003.11 - Deadline is enforced at acceptance

Criterion
A ballot can be accepted only when the poll is open and the transaction's acceptance time is strictly before the deadline, regardless of timer delay or an older authenticated page.
Proven by
RT003.9 - Deadline and close races.

AC003.12 - Pause stops voting without counting

Criterion
Pausing an open poll stops new ballots and revisions without freezing a result or creating count work. It may resume before the deadline under the approved policy.
Proven by
RT003.10 - Pause resume and manual close.

AC003.13 - Manual close counts paused polls

Criterion
From a paused poll, Close poll and count results freezes the eligible ballots and creates count work. A repeated close does not create another snapshot or result.
Proven by
RT003.10 - Pause resume and manual close.

AC003.14 - Scheduled close survives missed ticks

Criterion
The due-work command closes overdue open or paused polls from stored state, including after service downtime, and never accepts late ballots to compensate.
Proven by
RT003.9 - Deadline and close races; RT003.11 - Scheduled recovery and counting.

AC003.15 - Final ballots produce one durable result

Criterion
Counting uses one frozen snapshot and W002's engine. Restart and overlapping workers preserve the recorded decisions and at most one authoritative result per poll.
Proven by
RT003.11 - Scheduled recovery and counting; RT003.12 - Count interruption and replay.

AC003.16 - Moderator can inspect counted results

Criterion
The owning moderator can inspect the winners and count breakdown after counting, independently of announcement. Pending or failed counting is shown accurately.
Proven by
RT003.13 - Result visibility and zero turnout.

AC003.17 - Announcement follows the checkbox

Criterion
When checked, a committed result creates immediate announcement work for all invited addresses. When unchecked, no system announcement is created and the moderator announces externally.
Proven by
RT003.14 - Announcement choice and isolation.

AC003.18 - Delivery failure is recoverable and visible

Criterion
Email failures retain durable delivery outcomes and bounded retries without undoing voting or results. Exhausted retries are visible to the owning moderator.
Proven by
RT003.15 - Mail retry and failure visibility.

AC003.19 - Help is available without JavaScript

Criterion
The voting page provides plain-English voting and counting help without JavaScript, including the optional Wikipedia link and no national/electoral framing.
Proven by
RT003.16 - Rendered help and no-script forms; UT003.1 - Voting and help experience.

AC003.20 - Object access and private votes remain restricted

Criterion
A voter sees only their own ballot. Moderators see their own polls, aggregate turnout, results, and delivery status, without an individual-ballot or person-level voting-status interface. Disclose possible inference from small-poll results before voting; do not promise anonymity.
Proven by
RT003.1 - Moderator authentication and ownership; RT003.6 - Participant grant and session boundaries; RT003.13 - Result visibility and zero turnout.

AC003.21 - Forms protect state changes

Criterion
Mutating requests require valid authentication where applicable and CSRF protection. Tampered object IDs, oversized forms, and invalid transitions fail without partial writes.
Proven by
RT003.17 - Form and transaction security.

AC003.22 - Deployment remains Exodan-owned

Criterion
The application provides bounded process-due-work execution and timer intent, and packages its required assets/configuration under the accepted Exodan contract.
Proven by
RT003.11 - Scheduled recovery and counting; OT003.1 - Local full-poll and packaging review.

AC003.23 - Local end-to-end poll is usable

Criterion
A moderator and participants can complete the documented workflow locally, including multiple addresses, ballot revision, pause/close, counting, and the announcement choice.
Proven by
UT003.2 - Moderator and participant journey; OT003.1 - Local full-poll and packaging review.

RT003.11 - Scheduled recovery and counting

Covers

Setup
Executable and database containing overdue open/paused polls, a future poll, pending deliveries, and interrupted claims; real W002 engine for a fixed simple fixture.

Expected
Due polls close, future polls remain open, recorded work progresses in dependency order, and each logical operation is claimed safely. The command exits within its bound, returns non-zero on operational failure, and releases or expires claims for later recovery.
Work outcomes
No-work and a healthy bounded batch exit 0; transient/permanent attempt failures and execution-deadline cancellation exit 1 after preserving progress. A full 100-item batch with remaining work is successful, not a failure. Reclaim a claim whose token is still present but whose lease has expired; the stale holder cannot commit even if no replacement holder has yet run.
Summary oracle
For one overdue poll with two participants, one contact each, two A-first ballots, one winner, announcement checked and invitations already accepted, require exactly {"version":1,"closed":1,"counted":1,"no_votes":0,"smtp_accepted":2,"retrying":0,"failed":0,"cancelled":0} on stdout (JSON member order is immaterial). No-work produces the same fields with zero counters. A temporary mail failure increments failed and retrying, exits 1 and reports its safe diagnostic only on stderr. Startup failure produces failed=1 with other counters zero; failures after progress preserve the counters already earned. Check exactly these fields and non-negative integer values.

Edge Cases

  • Several invitations for one participant never create several ballots. Covered by AC003.3 - Several addresses share one participant and AC003.9 - Unlimited replacement of one vote.
  • A stale tab opened before pause or deadline cannot extend eligibility. Covered by AC003.11 - Deadline is enforced at acceptance.
  • A paused poll passing its deadline closes during reconciliation and cannot resume. Covered by AC003.14 - Scheduled close survives missed ticks.
  • An email scanner visiting a link does not consume the grant. The explicit exchange POST establishes the session. Covered by AC003.7 - Passwordless participant access.
  • A count interruption after a recorded lot does not redraw it. Covered by AC003.15 - Final ballots produce one durable result.
  • SMTP accepting a message immediately before a crash may lead to duplicate physical delivery, but never a second voting entitlement or authoritative result. Covered by AC003.18 - Delivery failure is recoverable and visible.
  • No votes means no invented winner, with the same announcement preference. Covered by AC003.16 - Moderator can inspect counted results and AC003.17 - Announcement follows the checkbox.

Successful mutations use POST/303 redirect/GET. Invalid fields return 422, stale versions/invalid transitions 409, over-limit bodies 413, CSRF failures 403, and unavailable storage 503. Unknown or unauthorized poll resources return the same 404 response, containing an ordinary link to the applicable access form. GET /moderator/polls without a session redirects to /moderator/login; object routes do not redirect differently according to whether the object exists. Unknown routes return 404 and unsupported methods 405.

Form decoding
Accept application/x-www-form-urlencoded POST bodies, with normal charset parameters, bounded before parsing. Reject other media types with 415, unknown fields and duplicated scalar fields with 422. Explicit participant and rank collections may repeat only their declared indexed fields; reject duplicate option or participant identifiers. Escape all echoed input. RT003.17 includes media-type, multiplicity and unknown-field cases.
Versions
Draft updates, participant edits, open, pause and resume carry the poll version from the rendered form. Check it after obtaining the write transaction and increment it on mutation. A repeat of an already-applied open or close returns its current status without new work; other stale requests return 409. Ballots use their independent version, with version 0 representing no accepted ballot.

Access and credentials

Configuration history
The former terse moderator/signing/SMTP key list is superseded by the complete schema and examples above.
  • Construct all absolute poll, moderator login, participant invitation, and replacement links from the supplied base_url loaded through W001's configuration boundary. Neither ADDR, request Host, nor forwarded headers may select a link destination. URL/domain validation and deployment policy belong to Exodan; this application consumes the configuration without duplicating those checks.
  • Sessions :: Use separate stvmoderator and stvparticipant cookies; the latter holds one active poll scope per browser. Each value is 32 random bytes encoded base64url without padding; store only its SHA-256 hash, purpose, scope and 12-hour absolute expiry. Cookies are host-only, HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax, path=/, with Secure controlled only by http.securecookies. Logout revokes both current sessions. Session cookies are never accepted as grants.
  • Grants :: Token format is base64url(payload) + dot + base64url(HMAC-SHA-256(payload)), without padding. Payload is versioned JSON containing v=1, keyid, purpose (moderator or participant), principal ID, poll/contact IDs when participant-scoped, issuedat/expiresat UTC Unix seconds and a 32-byte random nonce encoded base64url. Reject malformed, over-4096-byte, unknown-version, inactive-key and invalid-signature tokens before granting access. Compare MAC/hash in constant time and require the matching stored grant record to remain eligible. Moderator expiry is 15 minutes, participant expiry 24 hours; expiry equality is invalid.
  • Grant recovery :: Persist claims and token hash so the sender can reconstruct the same signed grant without storing a raw bearer URL. Before an attempt, a grant with less than five minutes remaining or a different active key ID is atomically superseded by a fresh grant under the same logical delivery. The old grant is revoked; no second logical invitation is created. Normal separately requested participant links may coexist until their expiry.
  • Key rotation :: Change auth.keyid with auth.signingkey and restart serve. Earlier-key grants cease to authenticate; pending mail gets fresh grants as above. Opaque existing sessions retain their own expiry and continue to check configured authority. Removing a moderator or changing their configured email invalidates grants/sessions tied to the previous configuration after restart; ownership records retain the stable ID.
  • One-use moderator consumption and session creation occur in one transaction. Participant grants remain reusable within their lifetime. Every use checks the current configured moderator or poll participant.
  • Grant/session lifetime is independent of voting eligibility. A valid session may display a closed poll's own-ballot status but cannot submit after closure.
  • All authentication responses and ballot pages use no-store caching and a no-referrer policy. Logs exclude query tokens, cookies, SMTP credentials, ballot content, and unnecessary personal data.
  • Link requests :: Accept replacements for configured moderators and contacts in open polls strictly before deadline. Paused/closed/nonexistent polls and unknown addresses receive the same 303 confirmation but queue nothing. Invitation work is held while paused and cancelled if still unsent at closing; announcements are independent of invitation cancellation.
  • Throttling :: Permit one accepted link request per normalized identity/purpose/poll per minute and five in a rolling 15 minutes. Also permit at most 1000 link-request submissions globally in a rolling 15 minutes, counting known and unknown identities before queueing. Above either limit, return the same generic confirmation and queue nothing. Store hashed request keys/timestamps, purge entries older than 15 minutes, and cap stored rate records at 1000. No request-IP or forwarded-header interpretation is required; the earlier undefined source-address limit is withdrawn in favour of these application/mail limits.
  • CSRF :: Every mutation requires a random 32-byte CSRF token bound to its session and verified against the stored hash. Public login/access forms use a distinct 15-minute pre-authentication session with host-only HttpOnly SameSite=Lax cookie and the configured Secure flag. Successful grant exchange rotates into the appropriate authenticated session. GET may establish this technical session but never consumes a grant or mutates poll/ballot state. No URL-supplied return destination is accepted; redirects use fixed application routes. Origin/Referer headers are neither authorization nor a separate filter: the session-bound token supplies CSRF protection, with SameSite as an additional browser control. No URL validation is added.
  • Technical-session lifecycle :: Remove expired session records during authentication and each sweep; never remove poll/ballot records in this cleanup. Reuse a valid presented pre-authentication session. Permit at most 1000 live pre-authentication sessions, enforced transactionally; a form request requiring another session receives 503 when full. A successful exchange revokes its pre-authentication session and replaces the presented same-purpose authenticated session. This bounds abandoned-form state without interpreting proxy or client-IP headers.

Poll state and input

States
draft, open, paused, closed. Counting status is separate: pending, awaiting_decision, succeeded, or failed; no_votes is a completed poll outcome without an engine count.
Transitions
draft->open; open->paused; paused->open only before deadline; open/paused->closed at deadline; paused->closed manually. No other transition is accepted.
Text
Question 1 through 500 characters; option label 1 through 200; participant display name optional up to 100. Treat these as plain text and escape on output. A draft may be incomplete; opening requires valid fields, at least two options, one participant, one address per participant, and a future deadline.
Options
Unique stable IDs and distinct trimmed labels within the poll; winner count 1 through option count.
Contacts
Support bare mailbox addresses validated by the standard mail parser. Reject display-name forms and delimiter-containing quoted forms in this comma/colon input format; do not invent a permissive email regex.
Draft participant edits
Apply the submitted list atomically. Copying creates new participant/contact identifiers and excludes all ballots, grants, and delivery outcomes.
Ballot form
One labelled rank input per option, the current ballot version, CSRF token, and submit action. Unranked fields are blank. Validate the complete submitted ranking before replacing anything.

Storage and consistency

Record Required ownership and constraint
Poll Owner moderator ID, question, deadline instant and display offset, winners, announcement preference, state/version, created/closed timestamps.
Option Poll ID, stable option ID, label, and display order; references cannot cross polls.
Poll participant Poll ID and stable participant ID; identity is local to the poll.
Contact Participant and poll association, delivery spelling and normalized email; normalized email is unique within a poll.
Grant and session Purpose, principal/scope, hashed token, claims/key ID for grants, validity and consumption/revocation data; moderator records capture their configured normalized email for later authority comparison. Secret signing material stays outside SQLite.
Ballot Unique poll/participant key, current version, ordered option IDs, and accepted timestamp. Keep only the effective revision in the first release.
Count snapshot Unique closed-poll input, explicit ballot order, opaque count-only ballot IDs, options, place count, schema/rule version, and input fingerprint.
Count decisions/result Unique sequence per snapshot and one authoritative result per snapshot; retain W002's structured record.
Work and delivery Unique logical operation key, type, due time, bounded claim/token, attempts, status, and safe failure classification. Recipient scope and associated grant ID are explicit; superseding a grant preserves the delivery ID.
Link-request limits Hashed identity/purpose/poll key and submission time; at most 1000 entries within a 15-minute window, with expired entries removed on request processing. No raw unknown email is retained.

Each ballot mutation begins a write transaction, rechecks authority/state, samples UTC time after obtaining the write boundary, checks version, and commits the replacement. Closing uses that same serialization boundary to freeze the input and insert count work. Database constraints remain the final protection against two effective ballots, cross-poll references, and duplicate results.

The close transaction assigns fresh count-only ballot IDs, removes participant/contact identifiers from the snapshot, and applies one uniform Fisher-Yates shuffle using cryptographic randomness. It persists that exact order with W002's schema=1 and rule=irish-guided-stv-v1 before any count runs. A failed close, including shuffle failure, rolls back the entire close transaction and leaves no snapshot or job. Manual close returns 503 for a safe retry; a scheduled close records an operational failure and may retry on the next sweep. After the deadline, the page says "Voting ended; results pending" even if the close has not committed. A committed snapshot is never reshuffled.

W002's unresolved candidate lots are answered by uniform cryptographic selection from its eligible list. Persist each request and answer, including its fingerprint and sequence, under the current work claim before replaying the engine. A unique snapshot/sequence constraint prevents competing answers; a retry uses the already committed answer. Lot-selection RNG failure after a snapshot exists produces a visible failed count, retaining that snapshot and earlier decisions without a new answer or result. This is reproducibility, not ballot anonymity.

Result projection
The engine's full record remains internal. Moderator HTML shows quota, aggregate turnout, per-option totals by count, elections/exclusions, aggregate transfers/exhaustion and option-level lot outcomes. It never returns snapshot JSON, ballot IDs/preferences, parcel membership or request fingerprints. Participant pages show only that participant's own current ballot and poll state.
Privacy presentation
Before voting, explain that one current ballot is stored per participant, moderators see aggregate results, and records are retained for review/list reuse. Include: "In a small poll, the results or turnout may allow someone to infer how a person voted or whether they took part." Do not promise anonymity: operators controlling storage can access the underlying associations. No individual-vote history or export route is introduced. Aggregate results without a group-size threshold were explicitly approved with this specification.

Due work and delivery

Command
stv-poll process-due-work uses the same configuration and database as serve. It reconciles due polls, advances counts, then dispatches due messages; it performs one bounded pass and exits.
Bounds
Maximum 60 seconds per invocation and 100 work items, including each close, count or delivery operation. Query in due-time/ID order, processing closing, counting and then delivery within the shared bound. A claim lasts 120 seconds; every mutation/acknowledgement checks its token, pending state and unexpired lease inside the transaction. Cancellation leaves unfinished work reclaimable; successful earlier operations remain committed.
Command outcomes
Help/version and invocation errors follow W001. No work or a healthy bounded batch exits 0; any attempted dependency/count/mail failure or execution-deadline cancellation exits 1 with a safe diagnostic. Existing terminal failures are not retried by each invocation and do not independently fail otherwise healthy sweeps. A full batch with remaining work exits 0; the next timer invocation resumes it. Emit one JSON stdout summary with version=1 and counts for closed, counted, novotes, smtpaccepted, retrying, failed and cancelled; diagnostics go to stderr.
Summary meanings
Emit exactly those eight fields, all non-negative integers, for this invocation only. closed counts committed closes (including zero turnout); counted counts committed engine results; novotes counts committed zero-turnout outcomes; smtpaccepted counts successful SMTP attempts; retrying counts attempts scheduled for retry; cancelled counts unsent deliveries cancelled by closing or expiry; failed counts failed attempted operations, including retryable failures, plus one for startup or execution-deadline failure. A retryable failure increments both failed and retrying. Startup failure emits failed=1 and all other counters zero. Invocation errors follow W001's exit 2/usage contract without a worker summary. Output contains no personal or secret values.
Scheduler
Declare a one-minute interval and this argument vector through Exodan's timer intent. The application creates no timer/cron/service definition.
Result commit
Store result, mark count work successful, and insert the logical announcement deliveries in one transaction. Never send before commit.
SMTP
Connect using the configured host/port and a 10-second overall attempt deadline. Require verified TLS through explicit implicit-TLS or required-STARTTLS mode; no opportunistic downgrade. Local plaintext SMTP is allowed only with an explicit development setting and a loopback destination.
Retry
Initial attempt plus at most five retries. After successive temporary failures, schedule 1, 2, 4, 8 and 16 minutes plus uniform jitter from 0 through 10% of that delay, persisted once with nextdue. Permanent recipient rejection, authentication rejection and TLS policy/certificate failures become terminal immediately; temporary SMTP 4xx, connection failure and timeout/ambiguous acceptance are retryable. Persist attempt count and outcome. A process crash during an attempt consumes that started attempt; lease expiry schedules its bounded retry rather than resetting the counter. Tests inject jitter and inspect its persisted range/reuse.
Attempt boundary
Commit the incremented attempt counter and in-flight state before SMTP. Sending happens outside transactions. A final ambiguous/crashed sixth attempt becomes terminal failed after lease expiry; it cannot cause an unlimited retry loop. Before each attempt, recheck the grant and invitation eligibility under the claim. Closing atomically cancels unsent invitation/replacement jobs; an SMTP attempt already in progress may still arrive, but the closed-poll eligibility check prevents voting.
Eligibility at send
Invitation/replacement attempts require an open poll and time strictly before its deadline; paused work is held, and elapsed/closed work is cancelled without another attempt. An already-started SMTP operation may finish after pause or close. Moderator login mail instead checks the currently configured moderator; result announcements require a committed result and the recorded checkbox setting.
Message construction
Use text/template for the exact invitation body. Invitation subject is Vote: [thing]; moderator and participant replacement subjects are Sign in to STV Poll and Return to [thing]. Replacement bodies contain one short instruction and the recipient's link. Strip CR/LF from subject substitutions, encode non-ASCII headers with MIME encoded words, and use UTF-8 text/plain bodies. Each message has exactly one envelope/header recipient; no contact list or credential appears in logs.
Visibility
Show safe delivery states per contact to the owner, distinguishing queued, SMTP-accepted, retrying, and failed. Do not call SMTP acceptance "delivered to inbox".
Logical uniqueness
Initial invitations are unique per poll/contact; announcements per result/contact. A repeated HTTP action or worker does not create another logical operation. Physical SMTP duplicates remain possible after ambiguous acceptance.
Failures
A count error becomes visible and retains its snapshot/decisions; no automatic repeated count error loop is run. Infrastructure interruption reclaims unfinished work. Do not silently replace an existing successful result.

Approved announcement copy

A successful announcement has subject Result: [thing] and this plain-text body:

The result for [thing] is:

[winners, one per line]

Thank you for voting.

A zero-turnout announcement uses the same subject and states:

No votes were received for [thing], so there is no winner.

Send each address its own message. The announcement does not include ballots, contact lists, or a public result link. Invitations remain exactly as in VISION.

Architecture impact

Security impact

Threat boundary
Public form/authentication endpoints, email bearer links, moderator ownership, participant identity, and persisted votes.
Data and secrets
The database retains personal contacts and ballot associations; do not promise anonymity. Consume Exodan's state-access boundary and disclose the approved retention/visibility policy in the UI; the application does not manage host ownership or permissions.
Access
Deny cross-owner/cross-poll access; derive participant identity from the scoped session, never a submitted email or arbitrary participant ID.
Mitigation
Atomic grant consumption, hashed sessions, explicit scope, CSRF, output escaping, bounded inputs, private pages, safe link origins, redacted logs, TLS mail, and dependency scanning.
Approval
Privacy, retention and announcement policies were included in the operator's original specification approval. This refresh preserves them; any later material change requires explicit sign-off.

Other relevant qualities

Reliability
Durable work, transactionally frozen inputs, persisted decisions, idempotent logical operations, and bounded recovery.
Performance
The stated limits bound first-release workload. Counts and network I/O never hold the database write transaction; no capacity claim substitutes for measured delivery evidence.
Operability
Owner-visible pending/failure states; structured CLI outcomes; serve and worker share the runtime contract. Use stopped-service SQLite backup/restore as the first documented consistency boundary, coordinated through Exodan rather than application-owned host scripts.
